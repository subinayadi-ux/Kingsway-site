How we build performance

Every build starts with a measurement and ends with one. Here's how we plan, test, fit and tune, and what you take home at the end.

The process

Whether it's a tune or a full turbo build, every job follows the same five steps, so you know what you're paying for before any spanners come out.

  1. Talk it throughWe ask how you use the car, what you want from it and what you want to spend. Then we check the engine is healthy enough to take more power, with a compression or leak-down test if needed.
  2. Baseline on the dynoWe measure power, torque and air-fuel ratio before touching anything. It shows where the car is now and catches problems a road drive won't.
  3. Written build planYou get a staged plan with parts, labour and the gain we expect at each stage. You choose how far to go, and nothing is added without your OK.
  4. Fit and tuneParts are fitted, then the ECU is tuned on the dyno under load, one change at a time, while we watch temperatures, knock and fuelling.
  5. Final runs and road testWe repeat the dyno runs, road test in traffic and on the open road, and check for leaks and fault codes before handover.

Dyno testing

A chassis dyno is a set of rollers the car drives on while strapped down. It lets us load the engine the way a hill or a hard overtake would, safely and repeatably, while measuring exactly what reaches the road.

How a dyno session runs

The car is warmed to normal temperature, strapped at all four corners and cooled with high-flow fans so the engine sees real airflow. We do at least three full-throttle runs and use the average, so one good or bad run doesn't skew the figures. Results are corrected for air temperature and pressure, so a hot day and a cold day can be compared fairly.

What we watch

Power and torque are only part of it. On every run we log air-fuel ratio, boost pressure, ignition timing, knock activity, and intake and coolant temperatures. A tune that makes big numbers but runs lean or pulls timing isn't one we'll hand back.

Build upgrades

We build in stages, and each stage is tuned and complete on its own. You can stop at any stage and still have a car that drives properly every day.

Stage 1

Intake, cat-back exhaust and an ECU tune. The best gain for the money, and the car stays quiet and easy to live with.

Stage 2

Front-mount intercooler, high-flow exhaust and fuel upgrades, with a retune to suit. Adds strong midrange and keeps temperatures down.

Stage 3

Turbo upgrade, with injectors, fuel pump, clutch and engine internals as needed. Planned as a full project with a set budget.

Supporting upgrades

More power needs to stop, turn and stay cool. We match brakes, suspension, cooling and the clutch to each stage, and tell you straight when something needs upgrading before we add power.

Some modifications need engineering sign-off to stay road legal in NSW. We'll tell you which ones before we start, so there are no surprises at rego time or with your insurer.

Parts repair and replacement

Diagnose first

We find the cause before replacing anything. That means scan tools, live data, smoke and pressure tests, and a road test that reproduces the fault. Swapping parts until it goes away costs you money and doesn't always fix it.

Repair or replace

If a part can be repaired properly, such as a leaking turbo oil line or a sticking calliper, we'll tell you what that costs next to a replacement. When it has to be replaced, we quote genuine and quality aftermarket options side by side.

You see what we see

We keep the old parts for you to look at and send photos of anything worn or broken before we order replacements. Parts and labour are covered by our workshop warranty.
